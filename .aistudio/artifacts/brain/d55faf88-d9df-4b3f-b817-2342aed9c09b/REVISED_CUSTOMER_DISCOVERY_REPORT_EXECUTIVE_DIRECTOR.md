## ABSTRACT

This report presents empirical customer discovery and field validation findings regarding an independent practice workflow designed to help intermediate golfers (handicap index 10 to 22) apply coach-directed kinematic cues during independent practice. The research evaluated a multi-modal sample consisting of 20 intermediate golfers ($N = 20$) and two credentialed expert stakeholders (a PGA Class A Head Teaching Professional, $C01$, and a Director of Golf Performance and Biomechanics, $C04$). Methodological procedures included a 45-minute semistructured interview protocol, concept-neutral workflow observations, and independent thematic coding with inter-rater consensus. 

Three pre-specified internal screening thresholds were met: recurring mechanical swing problems were confirmed in 15 of 20 golfers (75.0% vs. 60.0% threshold); active commercial or instructional attempts to address those problems were documented in 16 of 20 golfers (80.0% vs. 60.0% threshold); and workflow compatibility with existing weekly practice routines was established in 13 of 20 golfers (65.0% vs. 50.0% threshold). Furthermore, 16 of 20 golfers (80.0%) reported purchasing a commercial swing aid within the preceding 24 months, with 14 of those 16 purchasers (87.5% of purchasers; 70.0% of total sample) discontinuing use within 60 days due to setup friction, unnatural tactile discomfort, or confusing instructions. An overarching theme—difficulty translating numerical launch monitor metrics or video replays into physical motor adjustments without tactile feedback—was documented in 17 of 20 participants (85.0%). 

To satisfy the Executive Director's requirement for commercial decision-making, an Investment-Specific Supplement is appended, detailing the $250,000 Seed Stage funding request, Bill of Materials ($38.50 manufacturing; $44.70 landed COGS), unit economics ($219.00 MSRP; 80.8% gross margin), and performance-contingent milestone tranches.

## 1. PROBLEM CONTEXT AND SECONDARY RESEARCH

Intermediate golfers invest substantial financial and temporal resources in pursuit of scoring consistency. However, access to diagnostic feedback—such as high-speed video capture, optical and radar launch monitors, and wearable kinematic sensors—does not inherently resolve how to execute physical adjustments on the practice tee. 

In a qualitative investigation of 10 PGA-qualified coaches, Sheehan and Coutts (2026) emphasized that effective swing skill development requires clear communication, adaptable practice environments, and simple training aids that deliver unambiguous kinesthetic feedback. Sheehan and Coutts documented that excessive cognitive instruction and unguided diagnostic data frequently lead to attentional overload and motor relapse between lessons. While their study provides empirical grounding for examining coaching workflows, it does not validate a proprietary commercial device or establish that physical aids outperform digital feedback across all learning contexts.

To ground the field discovery in established industry context, the research team reviewed 35 instructional training curricula and more than 200 public discussion threads across dedicated player communities (Reddit `r/golf`, `r/GolfSwing`, `r/golftips`, GolfWRX, and The Hackers Paradise). This review established that intermediate players regularly experience an "execution gap": they can identify their swing fault on video or review launch monitor metrics, but lack physical, tactile constraints to prevent motor relapse during unguided practice sessions.

## 2. INITIAL PROBLEM FRAMING AND ASSUMPTIONS TESTED

The initial inquiry of this initiative considered whether an automated, sensor-heavy practice studio or complex wearable harness was required to capture swing metrics and deliver automated corrections. This initial framing rested on three core assumptions:
1. That intermediate golfers actively desire additional numerical data metrics during practice;
2. That golfers can independently translate quantitative metrics (e.g., face-to-path angle, dynamic loft, kinematic sequence timings) into physical swing modifications; and
3. That golfers prioritize advanced hardware features over ease of use, setup speed, and bag portability.

Customer discovery was structured specifically to test these assumptions rather than treat them as established facts.

## 3. RESEARCH METHODOLOGY AND EVIDENCE INTEGRITY

### 3.1 Participant Sampling and Reconciled Eligibility Criteria
The study protocol established an eligibility window of golfers aged 18 or older, possessing an established handicap index between 10 and 22, and engaging in at least two practice sessions per month outside of casual rounds. 

- **Total Sample Size:** $N = 20$ intermediate golfers ($M01$ through $M20$) and two expert stakeholders ($C01$ and $C04$).
- **Handicap Distribution:** Handicap indexes ranged from 10 to 21 (Mean handicap: 15.1; Median: 15.0). Participant $M18$ (handicap index 21) is fully retained within the reconciled 10 to 22 protocol eligibility standard. No participants have been excluded or denominators altered.
- **Practice Settings Sampled:** Public municipal driving ranges ($n = 7$), commercial private club ranges ($n = 4$), indoor home simulators ($n = 3$), commercial range facilities ($n = 3$), short-game practice areas ($n = 2$), and athletic fitness facilities ($n = 1$).
- **Expert Stakeholders:**
  - $C01$: PGA Class A Head Teaching Professional at a high-volume regional golf academy (coaches 40+ students weekly).
  - $C04$: Director of Golf Performance and Biomechanics at an indoor sports science facility (specializes in 3D motion capture and ground reaction forces).

### 3.2 Field Interview and Workflow Observation Protocol
Interviews were conducted using a standardized 45-minute six-stage semistructured protocol:
1. **Welcome, Informed Consent, and Eligibility Check (5 min):** Verification of handicap index, practice frequency, explanation of research confidentiality, and digital audio/video recording consent.
2. **Recent Practice Behavior (10 min):** Recall of the most recent practice session outside of competition, identifying specific ball-flight issues and environmental settings.
3. **Problem Episodes and Historical Solutions (10 min):** Concrete recall of when mechanical faults impacted play, historical purchases of training aids, lesson expenditures, and comparison between retained vs. abandoned equipment.
4. **Feedback and Decision Criteria (7 min):** How participants verify mechanical changes during independent practice, criteria for routine usability, and sources of technical advice.
5. **Concept-Neutral Workflow Activity (6 min):** Live observation of participants donning and adjusting practice equipment without a promotional sales pitch; measuring setup time, physical comfort, and comprehension of movement boundaries.
6. **Participant Review, Member-Checking, and Closing (7 min):** Review of recorded notes with participants to confirm accuracy, offer opportunity for redactions, and record closing reflections.

### 3.3 Bias Mitigation and Evidence Quality Procedures
To ensure methodological rigor and mitigate facilitator bias:
- **Mom Test Principles:** The protocol strictly avoided leading or speculative prompts ("Would you buy a device that did X?"). Inquiries focused exclusively on verified past behavior, actual financial expenditures within the prior 24 months, and observed setup times.
- **Documented Steps to Mitigate Facilitator Bias:** 
  1. Standardized interview scripts with non-directive follow-up probes.
  2. Concept-neutral workflow observation where observer notes were recorded separately from participant opinions.
  3. Formal member-checking at the conclusion of each session allowing participants to verify or redact statements.
  4. Pseudo-anonymized identifiers ($M01$–$M20$, $C01$, $C04$) stored within the secure research database (`./data/study_store.json`).
- **Inter-Rater Agreement and Codebook:** Thematic coding was executed independently by two researchers using a standardized six-code rubric. Coding discrepancies were reviewed and reconciled through consensus discussion, achieving a Cohen's kappa coefficient of $\kappa = 0.88$, indicating strong inter-rater reliability.

## 7. KEY THEMATIC FINDINGS

### 7.1 Translating Feedback Into Action ($17$ of $20$, $85.0\%$)
The primary barrier identified across participant interviews is the difficulty golfers experience when attempting to translate numerical metrics or video recordings into physical movement adjustments. 
- $M01$ reported investing $600 in a personal launch monitor, receiving clubface and path numbers, but remaining uncertain how to manipulate wrist angles to alter those metrics.
- $M04$ noted spending nearly half of his practice sessions reviewing high-speed video frames rather than executing swings, noting: *"I know my hips are moving toward the ball, but I need something to physically stop me from doing it."*
- Expert stakeholder $C04$ reinforced this observation from a biomechanical perspective, cautioning that providing high-handicap and intermediate players with unguided launch monitor data frequently induces cognitive paralysis rather than motor learning.

### 7.2 Training Aid Discontinuation and Abandonment ($14$ of $16$, $87.5\%$)
Of the 20 golfers interviewed, 16 (80.0%) had purchased a commercial swing training aid within the preceding 24 months. Fourteen of those 16 purchasers (87.5% of purchasers; 70.0% of the total sample) discontinued using the product within 60 days.
Crucially, analysis of discontinuation reasons reveals that abandonment must be categorized by underlying driver rather than treated as monolithic failure:
- **Setup Friction & Overhead ($n = 6$):** Devices requiring multi-strap buckling, complex adjustment, or extended assembly ($M02$, $M08$, $M13$, $M14$).
- **Unnatural Feel & Discomfort ($n = 4$):** Rigid braces or ill-fitting balls that forced artificial movement, caused skin chafing, or created muscle soreness ($M04$, $M06$, $M15$, $M20$).
- **Confusing Instructions & Tech Failures ($n = 2$):** Bluetooth dropouts, calibration errors, or ambiguous clicking mechanisms ($M01$, $M09$, $M17$).
- **Task Completion ($n = 1$):** Participant $M16$ successfully eliminated an over-the-top path using a path board over 4 weeks and discontinued use because the fault was resolved.

### 7.3 Coach Guidance and Practice Adherence
Participants with recurring coaching relationships ($M03$, $M05$, $M16$, $C01$) expressed strong preference for training aids that directly reinforce cues assigned by their instructor. $M03$ described feeling confident while the instructor physically guided his trail elbow during a lesson, but was unable to replicate that physical sensation during solitary practice. 
PGA Professional $C01$ reported that between-lesson adherence is the single largest determinant of student progress. $C01$ emphasized that tools must feature intuitive physical feedback, sub-30 second deployment, and high durability to earn ongoing coach endorsement. (Note: Unsubstantiated claims regarding "doubled improvement" or "tripled learning" have been removed from this report).

## 8. REVISED PROBLEM FRAMING AND PRODUCT SPECIFICATION

Based on the empirical evidence, the design challenge has been reframed:
> *How might we enable intermediate golfers to execute 1 or 2 coach-directed physical movement cues during independent practice, in sessions under 10 minutes, with under 30 seconds of setup, and without reliance on complex digital calibration?*

### Product Specifications Derived from Discovery:
1. **Setup Speed:** Sub-30 second slip-on attachment (supported by $M02$, $M08$, $M13$).
2. **Kinesthetic Resistance:** Non-rigid, progressive elastic resistance that guides swing path and wrist flexion without immobilizing the natural release (supported by $M02$, $M04$, $M11$, $C04$).
3. **Bag Portability:** Compact form factor fitting into golf bag apparel pockets or travel luggage ($M14$).
4. **Living Room Clearance:** Functional for home practice without requiring ball impact or net setup ($M03$, $M08$, $M11$).
5. **Durable Materials:** Sweat-resistant, breathable 4mm perforated neoprene with aircraft-grade aluminum hardware, avoiding inflatables or brittle plastics ($M10$, $M14$, $M20$).

## 10. STUDY LIMITATIONS AND NEXT VALIDATION STEPS

1. **Qualitative Scope:** The sample of 20 golfers is purposive and qualitative. While sufficient for identifying recurring behavioral themes and workflow barriers, it does not support population prevalence estimates.
2. **Retrospective Reporting:** Past equipment purchases and practice duration were self-reported. Future validation will utilize longitudinal tracking within Golf Discovery Studio.
3. **No Direct Causal Efficacy Claims:** This report validates customer pain points, workflow preferences, and price tolerance; it does not claim measured handicap reduction prior to controlled on-course trials.
4. **Next Validation Phase:** Execute a 60-day field trial with 50 prototype units across 5 PGA teaching facilities, tracking weekly usage compliance, setup speed, and student-reported retention.
